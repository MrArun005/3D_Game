# Reference: Shibuya Crossing (for Little Tokyo)

From Arun's photos (2026-09-23, five daytime shots of the scramble from street
level). Numbers are estimates from the photos. Each line is something
`world/tokyo.js`, `tokyoTypes.js` and `tokyoSigns.js` can be checked against.

## The set pieces round the crossing

- **QFRONT** (the Tsutaya building): a glass tower about 12 storeys tall with
  **rounded corners** in plan. It is wrapped in a dense grid of **white
  vertical fins**, about 0.6 m apart and full height. A **big screen is set
  into the fins** at mid-height, and a lattice crown sits on the roof. At the
  base: `TSUTAYA` in white capitals over the entrance, and a Starbucks glass
  box at level 2 overlooking the crossing.
- **The sign-stack building** (the Hisamitsu / DHC / DMM corner): the upper
  facade is almost entirely signs.
  - **Hisamitsu**: a rooftop pair of boards, blue over green, with white
    text and `サロンパス` on the green one.
  - **DHC**: a screen in a blue frame.
  - **DMM**: a large portrait screen below the DHC screen.
  - **UC**: a round white logo on a red board.
  - At street level: a blue `大盛堂書店` shop board.
- **Shibuhachi / the Yakult corner** (opposite QFRONT):
  - Rooftop: two **huge billboard screens** side by side, one of them
    **curved**.
  - Below them: stacked **horizontal lightboxes** in red, yellow and white
    (`プライス`, `コンタクト`, `ガスト`, `しゃぶ葉`).
  - A tall blue **vertical banner** runs down the corner.
  - Pink and white clinic boards sit lower down.
- **Down the axis**: the **109 cylinder** (a silver drum with the logo at the
  top, and posters on the drum); Magnet by Shibuya109, with a big dark
  rooftop screen; and a **yellow cylindrical ad drum** on a mirrored blue
  glass tower.
- **Glass towers** frame the edges of the view: a blue-mirror curtain wall
  with fine horizontal banding, and a grey one with vertical fins.

## The crossing itself

- **Four zebra crossings plus two diagonals**, all very wide (~8-10 m).
  Bars are ~0.45 m with equal gaps, bright white, worn at the edges.
- The asphalt is mid-dark grey and **visibly cracked** (fine crazing). It is
  not black.
- **Yellow tactile paving** at every kerb ramp. A **pinkish-red paved
  plaza** on the station corner.
- **Signals**: Japanese horizontal three-lamp heads on long cantilever arms
  over the road. A blue `渋谷駅前` name board sits on the arm, with blue/white
  arrow signs nearby. Blue temporary crowd barriers stand at the corners.
- **Street trees**: full green zelkova on two corners, softening the signs.

## Crowd

- Hundreds of people in each phase, crossing in every direction at once,
  diagonals included.
- Clothing is mostly neutral: black, white, beige, navy. Colour comes from
  bags and umbrellas.
- About one person in ten carries a **black parasol** in sun.
- Cyclists on red share-bikes.

## Light and colour (day)

- Clear, deep blue sky with cumulus. Hard sun and strong shadows on the
  crossing.
- The signs read as **flat saturated colour even by day**: blue, green, red,
  yellow and white panels. Screens show bright images.
- Buildings under the signs are pale grey or white. The colour is in the
  signage, not the walls.

## What this means for the game

1. A **QFRONT-style landmark** on the Tokyo cross node that carries the
   scramble: a rounded glass drum with a fin grid, an inset screen and a
   lattice crown.
2. A **sign-stack tower type**: the facade given over to boards and screens,
   with a two-tone rooftop board pair (blue over green).
3. **Rooftop screen pairs**, one of them curved, on the corner opposite the
   landmark.
4. A **cylindrical ad drum** (109 / yellow drum) as a skyline marker down the
   main axis.
5. Crossing polish: wider bars, cracked asphalt, tactile paving, cantilever
   signals with the station name board.
