# Reference: Regent Street, London (for the Kingsway frontages)

Arun's brief (2026-09-23): the look should move toward a real West End street --
Regent Street -- by day and at night. What follows is what the listing texts and
surveys actually say, collected from web search results (the heritage sites
themselves are blocked from the build container, so these are the search
engines' quotations of them; sources at the end). Numbers are the ones to check
the generator (`world/regent.js`) against.

## The rule that makes it read as ONE street

- Each block had to be designed with a **continuous unifying facade** to the
  street, **finished in Portland stone**, with a **uniform cornice level 66 feet
  (20.1 m) above pavement**, excluding dormers, turrets and mansard roofs.
- The street is **uniform five-storey** buildings; some dormers break the rule.

## The facade, bottom to top

- **Podium: ground floor + mezzanine** read as one base ("podium embracing ground
  floor and mezzanine"), then **three storeys above**, then a **steep dormered
  mansard** with a **rich modillion eaves cornice** and a **continuous range of
  dormers** in the roof.
- In the Quadrant (Blomfield, 1923-28): **shop-window bays, each with a mezzanine
  tier of three stone-framed windows, set between rustic piers of channel-jointed
  pulvinated courses**. Upper windows sit in **vertical bands of plain masonry,
  slightly projecting from the plain piers**, proportioned to the three storeys.
- 203-221 (Tanner / Darbyshire, c.1916-24): Portland stone ashlar, 4 storeys with
  ground + 1st as the podium, **attic storey and steep dormered mansard**; shop
  fronts **articulated by pilasters with engaged Doric columns carrying an
  entablature**.
- 249-259 (a Quadrant block): four tall storeys and **double attics in steep
  mansards and pavilion roofs**; **central stone dormers carried up from the
  attic with open pediments**.
- Block ends: **French mansard roofs / pavilions** mark the corners.
- Style: Beaux-Arts / French classicism (Blomfield) against Shaw's heavier
  Anglo-Italian Baroque at the Quadrant.

## Proportions to model (derived)

- Cornice at 20.1 m: podium ~7.5-8.0 m (shop ~5 m + mezzanine ~2.8 m), three
  upper storeys ~3.9-4.1 m each, cornice band ~0.9 m; mansard 2 storeys (~6 m)
  behind the cornice, dormers every bay.
- Bay rhythm ~4-5 m (a shop window per bay, three mezzanine lights over it).
- Pale Portland stone: warm off-white, slight variation per block; rustication
  on the podium piers only.

## At night

- The facades are **floodlit** (originally Meyer architectural floodlights, now
  LED): the stone glows softly from below, so the upper storeys read, not a black
  wall.
- Shop windows are the brightest thing at street level; buses and cabs streak
  light along the road; restaurants and theatres keep the pavements busy.
- November-January: the **Spirit of Christmas** overhead installation -- 30
  "spirits" of ~300,000 LEDs strung across the street (a seasonal option, not
  the default).

## From Arun's photos (2026-09-23, ten shots)

What the photos show that the listing texts do not. Each line is something the
generator can be checked against.

- **The Quadrant curve** (aerial and street shots): ONE continuous curved
  frontage, no gaps between buildings. The cornice, the storey bands and the
  mansard all run unbroken round the curve. At the far end the ground floor
  becomes an **arcade of round arches** carried on piers.
- **Roofline**: slate/lead-grey mansard (~#5c6168) with **a dormer on every
  bay** and **tall paired chimney stacks** every 3-4 bays. From street level
  the chimneys are what makes the skyline read as London.
- **Corner pavilions** end each block (five of the ten photos are corners):
  - the corner is **chamfered or rounded**, never a sharp 90 degree edge;
  - it rises one or two storeys above the cornice into an attic with an
    **oculus** (a round window, sometimes a clock);
  - it is capped by either a **lead cupola with a gilded ball finial** or a
    **verdigris copper dome** (~#8fc2a6) on a colonnaded drum;
  - a **two-storey arched window** sits over the corner entrance.
- **Stone**: pale Portland, warm off-white in sun (~#dcd5c7), cool grey under
  cloud (~#c9c6c0), soiled darker in the joints. **Channel-jointed
  rustication** (horizontal grooves ~0.5 m apart) runs the full height of the
  corner pavilions and across the podium elsewhere.
- **Podium**: black **steel-framed shop and mezzanine windows** with a
  small-pane grid (Crittall style), stone piers between, fascia lettering in
  small gold or white capitals on the stone. Some blocks (the green-domed
  corner) have a **red-brown granite** ground and mezzanine (~#6e3526) with
  arched windows.
- **Upper storeys**: sash windows with architraves, alternating triangular
  and segmental **pediments** on the first upper floor, black cast-iron
  **balconettes** on a few, a heavy modillion cornice, then a
  **balustrade with urns** along some parapets.
- **Street**: two lanes each way with kerbed central islands, light grey York
  stone pavements, black lamp posts and black signal poles, black cabs, New
  Routemaster red buses, **Union flags strung across the street on wires in
  rows** (Jubilee dressing) or the angel-wing "spirits" (Christmas).
